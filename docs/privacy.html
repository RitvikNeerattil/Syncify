Syncify

Privacy Policy

Last updated September 26, 2026

Syncify is a desktop app that runs on your computer. It has no servers, no accounts of its own, no analytics and no ads. The developer does not receive any of your data. Syncify is open source, so you can check all of this in the code.

What Syncify accesses from your Google account

When you choose "Sign in with Google", Syncify asks for:

  • Your email address (userinfo.email, openid), only to show which account you're signed in with.
  • Files Syncify creates in your Google Drive (drive.file). Syncify creates a folder called "Syncify" holding your songs and a small song list for each of your computers. It cannot see, read or change any other files in your Drive.

How that data is used

Only to sync your songs between your own computers. Everything goes directly between your computer and Google. Nothing is sent to the developer or to anyone else, and Google user data is never sold, used for advertising, or used to train AI models.

Syncify's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What's stored on your computer

  • Your settings and song list, in %APPDATA%\Syncify.
  • Your Google sign-in token, in Windows Credential Manager.
  • Your songs, in the music folder you choose.

Other services

Searching and downloading uses YouTube directly from your computer. To guess which part of a video title is the artist, Syncify may look up names on MusicBrainz (only the name is sent). On first launch Syncify downloads ffmpeg and Deno from their official GitHub releases. Those services have their own privacy policies.

Removing your data

  • Sign out in Syncify's Settings, or remove Syncify's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Delete the "Syncify" folder from your Google Drive.
  • Delete %APPDATA%\Syncify and your music folder on each computer.
